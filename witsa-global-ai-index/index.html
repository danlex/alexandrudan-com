What it measures

Ecosystems that were built, not announced.

The index scores AI ecosystem development, the structural conditions that decide whether AI produces durable national value, not the announcements or the strategy documents behind it. Scores are per-capita by design, so a small country building intensely is not buried by a large one, and each pillar runs 0 to 100 against the observed global frontier for that measure.

Infrastructure

Compute, connectivity, and the physical layer AI runs on.

Talent

Researchers, developers, and the people who build with AI.

Entrepreneurship

Startups, unicorns, and the pace of company formation.

Use Cases

Real adoption of AI inside organizations.

Data

Open data, datasets, and published models.

Policy

Governance, strategy, and AI readiness of the state.

Investments

Funding, greenfield capital, and financial commitment.

Why it is credible

Open sources, and open to challenge.

An independent host

WITSA carries no vendor interest and no government affiliation, which is the argument for letting it hold the measuring instrument.

Documented sources

Every indicator has a documented source, and every imputed value is flagged rather than hidden.

Contestable data

Every economy can contest a specific indicator value through a formal data review process run by the Secretariat.

A panel with authority

The full methodology was reviewed by 22 independent experts who had the authority to push back before they would validate it.

Source: WITSA Global AI Index 2026, confirmed pillar scores (final frozen run). Pillars run 0 to 100 on a per-capita basis.

Romania's clear strength is Data, at 87.7, one of the higher data scores in Europe, followed by Talent at 59.9 and Policy at 55.4. The constraints sit on the commercial and physical side: Entrepreneurship at 25.6, Investments at 31.4, and Infrastructure at 33.9. This is an economy with strong human capital and a strong data foundation whose AI ecosystem is held back by weak capital formation and a thin physical layer. For scale, the index is led by the United States at 82.2, Singapore at 82.0, and Sweden at 79.9. Luxembourg, where I chair the AI Committee of the Romania Luxembourg Business Forum, scores 59.2 overall, led by Talent at 83.2.

Why it matters

A national score is made of company decisions.

Romania's weak spots, turning capability and capital into deployed use cases, are exactly what I see inside individual firms as AI that stalls after the pilot. The panel measures where the country is weakest; my consulting and training work is aimed at the same pillar, Use Cases, one company at a time, getting teams past experiments and into production systems they actually use.
